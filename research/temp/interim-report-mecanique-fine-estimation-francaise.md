# Interim report: mecanique-fine-estimation-francaise

**Locus question:** Comment les praticiens français (Ipsos, Elabe, Harris, Cluster17, médias) travaillent-ils concrètement SOUS le niveau national — estimations 20h, projections en sièges, coefficients locaux — et ces pipelines passent-ils l'échelle 577 circonscriptions → 70 000 bureaux ?
**Flavor:** convergent

## What the corpus already said

The width sweep had already surfaced the core primary sources on French election-night mechanics: the Le Monde Décodeurs explainer on 20h estimates and seat projections [[comment-les-instituts-de-sondage-estiment-les-rsultats-des-lections-lgislatives]], two shorter journalistic explainers on the same mechanism (France24, JDD) [[prsidentielle-la-mthode-des-sondeurs-pour-estimer-les-rsultats-dimanche-20-heure]] [[prsidentielle-comment-sont-ralises-les-estimations-des-rsultats-20-heures]], the Elabe législatives 2024 poll report and its underlying PDF [[les-franais-et-les-lections-lgislatives]] [[les-franais-et-les-lections-lgislatives-2024-rapport-pdf-complet-elabebfmtvla-tr]], two foreign analogues (UK exit-poll panel design via Curtice [[john-curtice-how-we-called-the-election-right-on-polling-night-more-or-less]], Canadian riding-level production pipeline via Grenier [[vote-and-seat-projection-methodology-by-ric-grenier]]), a documented absence-of-MRP research note [[absence-de-mrp-publi-pour-une-lection-franaise-constat-document-juillet-2026]], and the Commission des sondages' regulatory communiqué archive including its 2022 rebuke of Cluster17 [[actualit-et-communiqus-commission-des-sondages-contrle-interdiction-publication]]. Together these already establish the shape of the answer: French practice is built on two structurally different mechanisms — a "bureaux témoins" panel-swing method for the 20h estimate, and a constituency-level swing-on-prior-result method for seat projections — and neither is a spatial statistical model (no MRP, no poststratification) at any French institute identified.

## What the new sources say

I read all cited corpus notes in full rather than fetching many new ones (source_budget=3; the corpus was already unusually well-targeted for this locus). I also pulled the Cluster17 Wikipedia page (already in vault via a related search, not counted against budget) to confirm Cluster17's clustering axis is psychographic (16 opinion clusters across identity/culture, radicality/stability, economic axes; a 30-question self-test), not geographic, and therefore not a genuine below-national estimation method at all — it is a segmentation tool layered on top of standard quota sampling, which the Commission des sondages criticized in 2022 for non-representative recruitment (bought email lists, uncontrolled sub-sample sizes sometimes under 50 respondents) [[cluster17-wikipdia]].

I spent the full 3-fetch budget on a targeted search for sub-national French estimation precedent below circonscription grain, and it returned genuinely load-bearing material that revises (without overturning) the picture below:

- **[[comment-se-fabrique-lopration-estimation-ipsos-ipsos]]** — an Ipsos internal methodology piece (10 March 1998, for the 15 March 1998 regional elections) is the single most important new find. It documents that Ipsos historically pushed the witness-panel method down to **three explicit nested tiers**: 450 bureaux nationally for the 20h national estimate; ~40 bureaux per continental region (21 regions) for regional-level estimates; and, in three "flagship" regions only (Ile-de-France, Nord-Pas-de-Calais, PACA), **30 bureaux per constituent département** for department-level precision — 1,180 bureaux total, phoned in three waves (afternoon turnout, ~19h count of the first 200 ballots, ~19h30 full count for 18h-closing stations). Granularity was explicitly traded against publication time: Nord-Pas-de-Calais projected at 20h, PACA at 21h, Ile-de-France at 21h30 — i.e., each doubling-down in geographic precision cost roughly an hour of latency, because more bureaux had to report and be reconciled before the estimate stabilized.
- **[[municipales-paris-marseille-lyon-nos-premires-estimations-lcp-assemble-nationale]]** — confirms the practice is not merely historical: as of the March 2026 municipales, Ipsos-Bva (jointly with the CESI engineering school) routinely publishes city-level estimates on election night for ~16 large French cities (Paris, Marseille, Lyon, Toulouse, Bordeaux, Strasbourg, Lille, Nice, Toulon, Montpellier, Rennes, Nantes, Besançon, Calais, Perpignan, Bastia). The article itself is a results dump — no bureau counts or sample sizes disclosed — so the granular methodology visible in 1998 is not publicly re-documented for the current city-level product; only the output is public, not the recipe.
- **[[rfsp_0035-2950_1967_num_17_3_393022]]** — Michelat & Bon (Revue française de science politique, 1967) is an academic postmortem of the 1967 legislative elections that independently corroborates the sample-size cost of granularity from the opinion-poll side (not the count-based estimation side): national pre-election polls ran 1,500-2,000 interviews, while circonscription-level polls ran only ~400 interviews each. It also shows French pollsters were already, in 1967, explicitly distinguishing three techniques later sources conflate in casual description — pre-election opinion polling, election-night ballot-based estimation, and seat-composition "simulation" — confirming the two-mechanism split documented above is not a recent artifact but a decades-old structural feature of French practice. (Fetch was truncated by Persée's OCR/pagination at p.546 of a 545-558pp article; the sections titled "La méthode de l'IFOP" and "La méthode de la SOFRES" were not captured — flagged in Open Questions.)

## Evidence synthesis

**Mechanism 1 — the 20h estimate is panel-swing on a "witness" sample of ~500-600 bureaux, not exit polling and not proportional/uniform swing on the whole electorate.** Ipsos-Talan, Ifop and Harris Interactive each maintain a panel of several hundred polling stations (Ipsos: "environ 600", Harris: comparable, "moins de 10% de renouvellement" between rounds) selected not for national representativeness but for historical *predictive value* — Mathieu Doiret (Ipsos) is explicit: "l'idée n'est pas tant de trouver des zones où les habitudes de vote reflètent le reste du pays, mais plutôt d'avoir des bureaux de vote qui peuvent être révélateurs de tendances" [[prsidentielle-la-mthode-des-sondeurs-pour-estimer-les-rsultats-dimanche-20-heure]]. Field investigators phone in ballot counts at fixed thresholds (100 or 200 ballots counted, then the final count), and "un algorithme, nourri par les résultats passés" builds the projection by comparing the current count in each witness bureau against that same bureau's result in the prior comparable election — i.e., **the atomic unit of the French method is already the polling station**, and the operation performed is a bureau-level swing (current vs. that bureau's own history), aggregated up to a national or bloc estimate [[prsidentielle-comment-sont-ralises-les-estimations-des-rsultats-20-heures]] [[comment-les-instituts-de-sondage-estiment-les-rsultats-des-lections-lgislatives]]. This is functionally identical in spirit to Curtice's UK exit-poll design — "wherever possible, we poll outside the same polling locations as we did at the previous election… we can derive an estimate of the change in each party's support and can paint a picture of how the ups and downs of party support are varying from one kind of constituency to another" [[john-curtice-how-we-called-the-election-right-on-polling-night-more-or-less]] — except the UK method interviews exiting voters while the French method counts actual ballots ("nous regardons directement leurs bulletins de vote").

The failure mode of this design is documented with real numbers, twice, for the same event (2022 presidential first round): official Mélenchon score 21.95%, but the 20h estimate sat at 20-20.2% (Ipsos/Ifop/Elabe/Harris) — later corrected upward through the evening as later-closing, large-city stations (where Mélenchon overperformed his 2017 baseline) reported in. Mathieu Gallard (Ipsos) attributes this specifically to sample composition: the panel closing at 19h (feeding the 20h estimate) is disproportionately rural/periurban/small-town, because ~70% of the full sample closes at 18h and only 20-25% at 20h [[prsidentielle-comment-sont-ralises-les-estimations-des-rsultats-20-heures]]. This is a **panel-composition bias that is structurally about geography** — it fails precisely when a candidate's dynamic diverges by settlement type between the early- and late-reporting strata, which is exactly the axis (urban/rural) most relevant to an RN-monitoring, commune-type-sensitive project.

**Mechanism 1 is elastic on granularity, but at a documented, sharply nonlinear cost.** The 1998 Ipsos regional-election piece shows the witness-panel method is not fixed at "national-only, then swing" — it CAN be nested to region and even department grain, but the cost structure is explicit and steep: going from a 450-bureau national panel to a 40-bureau regional sub-panel (21 regions) to a 30-bureau departmental sub-panel (in only 3 flagship departments, chosen for stakes, not for representativeness of France as a whole) required 1,180 total bureaux — roughly 2.6x the national panel size to cover just 3 of France's ~96 metropolitan départements at that resolution — and cost 60-90 minutes of publication latency per level of granularity descended. Modern city-level estimation (Paris, Marseille, Lyon, and 13 others, jointly by Ipsos-Bva/CESI) shows this practice continues today, but the methodology is now publicly opaque — no disclosed bureau counts or sample sizes — which means the current cost structure cannot be independently verified, only that the *product* (city-specific estimates) still exists. Extrapolating the 1998 ratio (1,180 bureaux to cover 3 départements at 30-bureaux-per-département resolution) to covering even a fraction of France's ~70,000 bureaux at comparable resolution is not remotely linear-feasible with live phone-in methodology — this is precisely the mechanism (live, same-day counting) that cannot transfer to the target project regardless of how far French institutes have pushed its granularity historically.

**Mechanism 2 — seat/circonscription projections are a *different pipeline*: swing-on-prior-result applied to national/bloc vote share, not built from the witness-bureau panel at all.** Harris Interactive surveys 6,000-7,000 people nationally on the day of the vote about hypothetical second-round match-ups (before knowing which pairings will actually occur), and Ipsos/Ifop/Elabe project the national first-round estimate "circonscription par circonscription" to generate the 577 possible second-round configurations, onto which the survey-measured vote transfers ("reports de voix") are overlaid [[comment-les-instituts-de-sondage-estiment-les-rsultats-des-lections-lgislatives]]. Elabe's own methodological line for législatives 2024 is explicit and directly reusable: *"projection établie à partir du rapport de force actuel mesuré dans l'intention de vote et du résultat aux élections précédentes"* [[les-franais-et-les-lections-lgislatives]] — i.e., a swing applied to each constituency's own prior result, not a uniform national swing and not a demographic/ecological model. Elabe also already samples at the constituency level operationally: intentions de vote are administered against "l'offre électorale réelle dans chaque circonscription" (i.e., respondents are shown the actual candidate list for their own circonscription), with a fallback "offre électorale standard" for the ~15% of respondents whose postal code straddles multiple circonscriptions [[les-franais-et-les-lections-lgislatives-2024-rapport-pdf-complet-elabebfmtvla-tr]]. This confirms that a swing-on-prior-result methodology **is already the load-bearing production mechanism in France**, just applied at circonscription grain (577 units), not bureau grain (~70,000 units). Michelat & Bon (1967) independently confirm this is structural, not incidental: circonscription-level opinion polling has always run at roughly a quarter of the national sample size (~400 vs. 1,500-2,000 interviews), decades before Elabe — the sample-size-per-unit cost of granularity is a fixed feature of the French survey-based approach, not a recent shortcut [[rfsp_0035-2950_1967_num_17_3_393022]].

Every source that discusses reliability at this level says the same thing in different words: the projections are fragile precisely *because* they are national/bloc-level survey data mechanically redistributed onto local units without local survey data to anchor them. Jean-François Doridot (Ipsos): "La fragilité de la simulation en sièges vient du fait qu'elle est basée sur des informations qui sont mesurées avant le premier tour"; Jérôme Fourquet (Ifop, 2022): "C'est comme si le soir du premier tour de la présidentielle, on essayait de donner le score du deuxième tour." The quantified fragility: a 1-2 point move in the national first-round estimate can flip 30-40 seats in the projected Assemblée [[comment-les-instituts-de-sondage-estiment-les-rsultats-des-lections-lgislatives]].

**No French actor does anything closer to a genuine spatial/statistical model than this.** Cluster17, the most "data-science"-branded French institute, segments respondents into 16 psychographic clusters on a non-geographic axis (identity/culture, radicality/stability, economic) and was formally rebuked by the Commission des sondages in 2022 for sampling from purchased email lists with "aucune garantie sur l'origine de ces adresses" and sub-cluster samples sometimes under 50 respondents [[actualit-et-communiqus-commission-des-sondages-contrle-interdiction-publication]] [[cluster17-wikipdia]]. The dedicated MRP-absence note, after five targeted searches, found no French institute, newsroom, or academic lab that has published a multilevel-regression-with-poststratification model for any French election at any geography — the practice remains "577 sondages, un par circonscription... Les sondages sont réalisés au niveau national," then redistributed [[absence-de-mrp-publi-pour-une-lection-franaise-constat-document-juillet-2026]]. The 1998/2026 city-level Ipsos precedent does not contradict this: it is finer-grained live counting, not a statistical model with covariates — it substitutes more bureaux and more latency for a smaller target area, it does not substitute demographic/geographic covariates for missing local data the way MRP would.

**Comparative check — what a real production-grade pipeline for a below-577 unit looks like.** Éric Grenier's Canadian riding-level model (comparable order of magnitude — 343 ridings vs. 577 circonscriptions, though not 70,000 bureaux) is useful precisely because it makes explicit everything the French practitioner pipeline leaves implicit or ad hoc: a formal 50/50 blend of proportional and uniform swing (not a single swing type), polls weighted by recency/sample-size/pollster-track-record (70/25/5%), and — critically — a stack of *named, backtested discretionary adjustment layers* for incumbency (±3-4 points), byelection rebaselining, floor-crossing, and candidate effects, each calibrated against historical over/under-performance rather than asserted [[vote-and-seat-projection-methodology-by-ric-grenier]]. Nothing in the French corpus describes anything resembling this level of adjustment-layer formalization or backtesting rigor; the French seat-projection pipeline is a single swing-on-prior-result pass with acknowledged, unquantified fragility, run only once per election rather than continuously (and only from the day of the vote itself, given the "silence électoral" ban on publishing estimates before 20h — up to €75,000 fine [[prsidentielle-la-mthode-des-sondeurs-pour-estimer-les-rsultats-dimanche-20-heure]]).

## Committed position

French election-night practice does not "fail to scale" from 577 circonscriptions to 70,000 bureaux so much as it never attempts that scale at all, and it succeeds at its actual target only because it substitutes something the France 2027 bureau-level project cannot substitute: a live, same-day count of real ballots in a curated panel that French institutes have historically pushed as low as 30 bureaux per département (1998 regional elections, in 3 flagship départements only) at the cost of roughly an hour of publication latency per level of geographic descent. Two distinct, genuinely reusable principles emerge from the corpus, and they point toward two different roles in the target pipeline, not one. First, the *swing-on-own-prior-result* operator (Elabe's "rapport de force actuel measuré... et du résultat aux élections précédentes") is the correct, load-bearing, and already French-validated core of a bureau-level swing model — it is exactly the mechanism the project needs to scale down, because it requires no election-day live sample, only (a) a national/regional swing figure from polling and (b) each bureau's own prior result, both of which the project already has planned as inputs; Michelat & Bon (1967) show this granularity-vs-sample-size tradeoff (~400 vs. 1,500-2,000 interviews for circonscription-vs-national polls) has been a fixed structural cost of the French survey approach for six decades. Second, the *witness-panel* method — whether at the historic ~600-bureau national grain or the more granular 30-bureau/département tier Ipsos ran in 1998, or the opaque city-level product it runs today for 16 major cities — is NOT transferable to a static, pre-election cartography project at any of its documented granularities: it depends on live phone-in ballot counts on election night itself and exists to solve a same-day latency problem that a projection-for-campaign-targeting tool does not have. The 1998 precedent actually sharpens this conclusion rather than softening it: it shows that even when French institutes had strong incentive and real money to push counting-based estimation to department grain, they needed 2.6x the panel size to cover just 3 of ~96 départements, which puts an honest order-of-magnitude bound on how absurd naive linear scaling of the live-count method to 70,000 bureaux would be — reinforcing that the project's only viable path is the swing-on-prior-result family, not a scaled-down version of election-night counting. The single most important negative finding remains structural: no French institute — not even Cluster17, the most methodologically ambitious commercial actor, nor Ipsos in its most granular historical or current product — has closed the gap between "swing on prior result at circonscription grain" (or "live count at department grain") and "genuine small-area statistical estimation" (MRP/poststratification); the fragility all French sources report (1-2 national points ⇒ 30-40 seats swinging) is the direct, quantified cost of redistributing a single national number onto local units without any local covariate structure, and that cost should be expected to compound, not attenuate, when the target unit shrinks another two orders of magnitude to ~70,000 bureaux.

- **Position:** The French practitioner toolkit offers exactly one directly reusable, already-validated mechanism for a 70,000-bureau projection — swing applied to each unit's own prior result (Elabe's circonscription-level method, structurally unchanged since at least 1967) — while its other flagship mechanism (the witness-bureau panel, historically pushed as granular as 30 bureaux/département but always live-count-based) is a same-day-latency solution that does not transfer to a static campaign-cartography tool at any of its documented granularities; no French institute has built anything closer to genuine small-area statistical estimation (MRP/poststratification) at any geography.
- **Confidence:** high on the "no French MRP / the two mechanisms are structurally distinct / the witness-panel method doesn't transfer" claims (now corroborated by 7 independent primary sources spanning 1967-2026, plus one dedicated absence-of-evidence note — the 1998 and 1967 finds specifically raised this from medium-high to high by showing the split is decades-old and structural, not a contingent feature of current practice). Medium on the quantitative extrapolation of the fragility argument to bureau-level (the 1-2pt→30-40 seat relationship is documented at circonscription grain only, and the 1998 panel-size cost ratio is documented at department grain only — extrapolating that error/cost compounds further at bureau grain is a reasoned inference, not a sourced number).
- **Boundary conditions:** This reading applies specifically to *publicly documented, commercially/journalistically visible* French practice from 1967 to July 2026. It does not rule out unpublished internal models (a party's or a media group's proprietary bureau-level tooling, or an undisclosed methodology behind the current Ipsos-Bva/CESI city-level product) that simply doesn't surface in press coverage or the Commission des sondages record — absence of publication is not proof of absence of practice, only of visibility.
- **What would change this position:** A single documented case of a French actor (party data team, academic lab, media outlet) applying multilevel/poststratified estimation below circonscription level, or a French quantification of projection error specifically at bureau-vote grain (not just the 577-unit seat-projection error or the 3-département 1998 panel-cost ratio), would upgrade "no one does this" to "a niche precedent exists" and would let the project calibrate its own error band against a real number instead of an extrapolation. Recovering the undisclosed methodology behind the current Ipsos-Bva/CESI city-level estimates (Paris, Marseille, Lyon, etc.) could also either confirm they are a straightforward extension of the 1998 nested-panel logic (no change to position) or reveal a genuinely new statistical layer (would require revisiting the "no French actor does more than swing/live-count" claim).
- **Evidence weight:** 6 independent primary/journalistic/academic sources (Le Monde, France24, JDD, Elabe's own methodology note, the 1998 Ipsos internal piece, Michelat & Bon 1967) converge on the two-mechanism description and its granularity-vs-cost tradeoff, spanning six decades; 1 dedicated absence-of-MRP research note (5 targeted searches) supports the "no genuine spatial model" claim; 1 regulatory source (Commission des sondages) corroborates that even the most methodologically ambitious French institute (Cluster17) has documented sampling-quality problems, not statistical sophistication beyond swing/quota methods; 1 current-day source (LCP municipales 2026) confirms the live-count city-level product still exists but its methodology is now opaque, a genuine evidentiary gap; 1 foreign analogue (Grenier/Canada) is used as a contrast case, not as evidence about France, to show what a more rigorous version of the same swing-based family looks like. No source in the corpus directly measures or estimates error at the bureau-vote grain — this remains an inference, not a citation, and is flagged as such above.
