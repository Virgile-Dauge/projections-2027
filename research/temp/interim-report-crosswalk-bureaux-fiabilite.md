# Interim report: crosswalk-bureaux-fiabilite

**Locus question:** Le crosswalk des identifiants de bureaux de vote (REU, table-bv-reu.csv) est-il suffisamment stable entre 2022 et 2024/2027 pour une jointure inter-scrutins fiable, ou la maille bureau est-elle structurellement instable ?
**Flavor:** dialectical

## What the corpus already said

The width sweep had already assembled the raw materials but not the number the locus asks for. [[jeu-de-donnes-donnes-des-lections-agrges-datagouvfr]] documents that the aggregated data.gouv.fr elections dataset joins to INSEE's REU "par l'intermédiaire de la table de conversion des identifiants des bureaux de vote (table-bv-reu.csv)." [[jeu-de-donnes-bureaux-de-vote-et-adresses-de-leurs-lecteurs-datagouvfr]] carries INSEE's own structural warning: "La numérotation des bureaux de vote relève de la compétence des communes, sans norme de codage stricte." [[une-approche-de-reconstruction-automatique-de-la-gomtrie-des-bureaux-de-vote-mak]] (Makina Corpus, 2020) establishes *why*: a bureau de vote is legally a commune-level rattachement of electoral addresses defined by arrêté préfectoral, not a geographic zone — some communes describe it as street-range lists (often not closed/contiguous), some as whole-commune, some as alphabetical elector splits. [[thomas-piketty]] (Cagé & Piketty) is the sharpest prior signal of instability: they explicitly abandoned bureau-level analysis for their 1848–2022 panel because "la cartographie des bureaux de vote a beaucoup changé sans laisser de trace homogène sur longue période." The two contour-reconstruction tools ([[github-datagouvbureau-vote-preparatory-work-for-the-publication-of-polling-place]], [[github-inseefrlabmapvotr-package-de-production-de-contours-de-bureaux-de-vote-pa]]) both use Voronoi tessellation on REU addresses and both state explicitly "il n'y a pas unicité des contours" — convergent methods, not identical outputs. [[a-vot-chaque-bureau-de-vote-ses-lecteurs]] provides the one empirical robustness check available (Strasbourg): where contours diverge, the underlying population aggregates (median income) stay nearly identical. US analogues ([[united-states-precinct-boundaries-and-statewide-partisan-election-results-scient]], [[changing-precinct-boundaries-who-is-affected-and-electoral]], [[github-mgggmaup-the-geospatial-toolkit-for-redistricting-data-github]]) establish that precinct churn is the administrative norm, not an anomaly, and that naive reaggregation across a redraw can silently drop double-digit percentages of votes. None of this quantified the actual French bureau-ID churn rate — that gap is what this investigation targeted.

## What the new sources say

**[[traitement-adresses-reudocmethodologiehtml-at-main-inseefrlabtraitement-adresses]]** — the official INSEE/Etalab methodology document (June 2023) for the REU↔MIOM crosswalk — is the load-bearing find. It gives the number the corpus lacked: **"Il subsiste toutefois quelques cas (3 000 bureaux de vote sur 69 000), où ces identifiants sont différents"** between the REU and the Ministry of Interior's own election-results system, as of the September 2022 REU extraction. It documents the matching cascade explicitly: (1) a classic join on `{code_commune}_{code_bv}` after cleaning/leading-zero stripping matches "~66k sur un total de 69k"; (2) arrondissement conversion rules for Paris/Lyon/Marseille add "~1.6k"; (3) automatic association of communes with a single bureau adds "~400"; (4) manual eyeballing of "evident" but non-automatable renumbering patterns (e.g. `[1,2,3…]` vs `[101,102,103…]`) recovers "~300 bv rattrapés." After all four passes, residual failures are "environ 130 bureaux de vote côté REU" and "près de 250 côté SIE" (~0.2%–0.4%), concentrated in three communes — Belfort, Troyes, Dieppe — attributed to bureau splits not mirrored on one side, or commune boundary changes. Critically, the document self-labels the table as **"une table de correspondance expérimentale et ponctuelle,"** states "Ces correspondances ne revêtent aucun caractère officiel," "n'est donc pas fiable à 100%," and warns "les différents référentiels [peuvent] changer avec le temps" — i.e. it was built once against a single snapshot, not maintained as a versioned per-cycle artifact.

**[[jeu-de-donnes-donnes-des-lections-agrges-datagouvfr-2]]** — the data.gouv.fr discussions tab (17 threads, 4 closed) for the aggregated elections dataset — supplies real, dated evidence of the join breaking *in production, across cycles*, independent of the REU step. A user reports (Feb 2026): **"Certains bureaux de vote ne correspondent plus avec ceux données par le ministère de l'intérieur. Par exemple, aux législatives 2024, à Montbéliard, on a des 0X01, 0X02… au lieu de 0E11, 0E321 au tour 1."** The maintainer confirms and patches it ad hoc; the user later reports (three days on) that the fix was partial ("Il y a aussi encore des problèmes de bureau en 0X*** au lieu de 0E*** pour Montbéliard dans candidats_results"). A separate thread traces the same commune's bureau code corruption back to municipales 2020 ("`25388_0,00E+00`" vs "`25388_0000`" for législatives 2024) — a formatting artifact from one scrutin's source file silently propagating and colliding across cycles. Recurring DOM-TOM prefix collisions (Mayotte `ZM`→`975` instead of `976`; Nouvelle-Calédonie, Polynésie, Wallis-et-Futuna similarly mismapped, colliding with real métropole department codes) needed two more rounds of user-reported fixes in Feb 2026. A maintainer also confirms directly: **"Le lien entre bureau de vote et circonscription n'étant pas simple."**

**[[datagouvfr_data_pipelinesdata_processingelectionsaggregationtask_functionspy-at]]** and its companion README are the mechanism confirmation. The actual Airflow DAG code that builds the aggregated dataset does **not perform the REU/MIOM join at all** — its own comment says: *"getting preprocessed resources, the magic is there (when creating standardized files), here we only concatenate them."* The harmonization logic lives upstream in a decentralized `community_resources` process, not an audited/versioned pipeline step. The README confirms the job runs **manually**, triggered ad hoc after each MIOM publication — not a scheduled, continuously-validated pipeline. This directly explains why mismatches (Montbéliard, DOM-TOM) surface as user bug reports months after publication rather than being caught by automated validation before release.

**[[issues-inseefrlabmapvotr-github]]** / **[[issues-inseefrlabmapvotr-github-2]]** — mapvotr's closed-issue list confirms the existence of an internal `decouplage_bv` (bureau-splitting) function in INSEE's own contour-generation tooling, i.e. bureau splitting is a known, named, handled case in the geometry pipeline — indirect confirmation that splits are common enough to need dedicated code, though the issue-listing page doesn't expose the mechanics.

## Evidence synthesis

Two numbers now anchor the debate, and they answer different questions. The **~4.3% (3,000/69,000) REU↔MIOM ID mismatch** is a *cross-referential* snapshot gap: two administrative systems (INSEE's electoral register and the Ministry's results system) disagreeing about a bureau's ID *at the same point in time* (September 2022). The **Montbéliard case** (0E11/0E321 → 0X01/0X02 between municipales 2020 and législatives 2024) is a *longitudinal* churn event: the Ministry's own numbering system disagreeing with itself across election cycles. These are not the same failure mode, and the corpus's original question — "is the maille bureau stable enough for 2022→2024/2027 joins" — is really asking about the second, unmeasured one. No source in the corpus (original or newly fetched) provides a systematic longitudinal churn rate comparable to VEST's "one-sixth of geographic units redraw precincts every cycle" figure for the US. The Montbéliard/DOM-TOM cases are the only concrete, dated instances of cross-cycle bureau-ID instability in the French corpus, and they surfaced via user bug reports, not systematic QA — meaning the true rate is almost certainly undercounted in the public discussion record (most communes with silent renumbering never get reported because no user happens to notice).

### Position A: the crosswalk is fit for purpose

The matching cascade in `methodologie.html` closes to 98.9%+ coverage (66k + 1.6k + 400 + 300 = 68,300 / 69,000) using deterministic rules, with residual failures concentrated in exactly 3 identifiable communes. Two independent teams (Etalab's `bureau-vote` and INSEE's `mapvotr`) reconstruct convergent geometric contours from the same REU address base using the same Voronoi method, and the one empirical validation available (Strasbourg, in [[a-vot-chaque-bureau-de-vote-ses-lecteurs]]) shows that even where contours *do* diverge, the resulting socio-demographic aggregates (median income) are nearly identical — meaning small ID/boundary noise washes out at the level that matters for downstream analysis. The data.gouv.fr aggregated dataset is under active, responsive maintenance: every reported mismatch (Montbéliard, DOM-TOM prefixes, missing nuances) was patched within days of being reported, and updates continue in 2026 (municipales 2026 integration visible in the discussion log). For the project's stated purpose — militant targeting, not exact vote-share prediction — a ~95-99% correctly-joined base with known, geographically concentrated failure zones is a workable substrate, not a blocker.

### Position B: the maille bureau is structurally unstable for inter-cycle joins

The methodology document's own language is unambiguous: the table is **"expérimentale," "ponctuelle,"** carries **"aucun caractère officiel,"** is explicitly **"pas fiable à 100%,"** and warns the underlying référentiels **"peuvent changer avec le temps."** This is INSEE and Etalab telling users, in writing, not to treat it as a stable long-term key. The REU itself is refreshed only every 5 years (single extraction: September 2022) while bureau perimeters are re-arrêté'd by communes on an annual/election-driven basis — a structural mismatch in update cadence between the crosswalk's reference data and the entity it's trying to track. The aggregation pipeline that actually produces the multi-scrutin dataset performs *no join at all* in an audited, versioned way; the harmonization is decentralized, manual, and reactive, which is exactly the production pattern that let a 2020 formatting bug (`25388_0,00E+00`) silently corrupt Montbéliard's bureau codes through the 2024 législatives, and why DOM-TOM prefix collisions needed three rounds of community bug reports in a single month (Feb 2026) to close. Root cause: numbering is a commune competency with no coding norm — nothing prevents (and Makina Corpus's arrêté analysis confirms nothing does prevent) a commune from renumbering, splitting, or merging bureaux between any two scrutins, and there is no national registry that timestamps or versions those changes for researchers. Cagé & Piketty — the most resourced, most ambitious French electoral-panel project to date — looked at exactly this problem over a much longer horizon and concluded it could not be solved reliably below the commune level. The US analogues are the closest available base rate for what "normal" looks like: ~1/6 of geographic units redraw precincts *every single cycle* as routine administrative practice, and even VEST — a multi-cycle, well-funded, methodologically mature project — still needs dasymetric reallocation and largest-remainder proration (`maup.prorate()`) to avoid silently losing double-digit percentages of votes across a redraw. There is no reason to expect French communes, with a weaker national data infrastructure and no equivalent of VEST's institutional practice, to be more stable than US counties.

## Committed position

The evidence resolves into a scope distinction, not a coin flip: the crosswalk is trustworthy as a **cross-referential key within a single administrative snapshot** (REU-to-MIOM matching at ~98–99%, sufficient to attach one election's results to REU geography/addresses) but there is **no evidence anywhere in the corpus of a maintained, versioned crosswalk that tracks bureau-ID changes across election cycles** — and the two concrete pieces of longitudinal evidence found (Montbéliard's 2020→2024 code drift, and the pipeline's admission that harmonization is manual/reactive rather than continuously validated) both point toward real, unmeasured churn rather than a stable base. Given that the project's actual need is a 2022→2024→2027 panel, not a single-snapshot join, the burden of proof should sit with anyone proposing to join bureau IDs directly across cycles without a validation step — and the practical fix is cheap: before trusting any bureau-level panel across scrutins, cross-tabulate bureau *counts* per commune across each pair of consecutive scrutins already present in the aggregated dataset (`general-results.csv`, filterable on `id_election` and `code_commune` — no REU step required for this check) and flag any commune where the count changes as structurally unstable for that period; those communes should fall back to commune-level aggregation (Cagé-Piketty's approach) or population-weighted dasymetric reallocation (`maup.prorate()`, weighted by REU electeur counts, not area) rather than a raw-ID join. Communes with stable bureau counts across all four scrutins (2022 pres, 2022 legi, 2024 legi, 2024 euro) can be treated as safe for bureau-level panel construction with high confidence; communes that show any count change should be treated as unsafe by default until manually reconciled, following the Belfort/Troyes/Dieppe precedent in the methodology document.

- **Position:** The bureau-vote ID/crosswalk is reliable for single-snapshot REU↔MIOM joins (~98–99% match) but is not a stable, maintained key for inter-cycle (2022→2024→2027) panels — no source demonstrates cycle-over-cycle ID stability, and the two dated cases found (Montbéliard, DOM-TOM prefixes) show real, unpatched-for-months drift within the Ministry's own numbering. Projects should build a per-commune bureau-count-stability check as a mandatory first step and fall back to commune-level aggregation or population-weighted proration wherever counts diverge.
- **Confidence:** medium-high on the qualitative claim (raw cross-cycle ID joins are unsafe without validation); low on any specific numeric churn rate — no source quantifies the 2022→2024/2027 longitudinal rate directly, so any percentage beyond the documented ~4.3% single-snapshot mismatch and the isolated Montbéliard/DOM-TOM cases would be extrapolation.
- **Boundary conditions:** This applies specifically to *bureau-level ID joins across election cycles*. Within a single scrutin, using the aggregated dataset's own `code_commune`+`code_bv` (or `id_brut_miom`) is reliable (that's exactly what the aggregation pipeline does, uncontested in the corpus). The REU↔MIOM crosswalk specifically (needed only when overlaying REU/BAN socio-demographic address data, not for pure inter-election result panels) is separately weaker (~98.9% best case, self-described as experimental/non-official) and should not be treated as authoritative outside the September 2022 snapshot it was built against.
- **What would change this position:** A systematic, election-by-election comparison of bureau counts and IDs per commune across 2022 pres / 2022 legi / 2024 legi / 2024 euro (the protocol above) that shows >97-98% of communes have zero bureau-count change across all four scrutins would substantially strengthen Position A and shift this toward "safe by default with narrow exceptions." Conversely, if that same check shows double-digit percentages of communes (weighted by registered voters) with count changes, it would confirm Position B's instability claim at a scale closer to the US 1/6-per-cycle base rate.
- **Evidence weight:** 1 primary methodological source with an exact snapshot mismatch number (INSEE methodologie.html, 3,000/69,000) supports Position A's precision claim but its own caveats support Position B's instability claim; 1 production discussion thread with 2 dated, unresolved-for-weeks cross-cycle ID drift cases supports Position B; 1 pipeline source code review confirms the harmonization process is manual/non-versioned, supporting Position B's mechanism argument; 2 academic/institutional precedents (Cagé-Piketty's explicit abandonment of bureau-level panels; 3 US precinct-boundary studies showing routine, structural churn) support Position B by analogy; 1 empirical robustness check (Strasbourg contour comparison) supports Position A's "noise washes out" claim but only for geometric contours, not ID matching. No source directly measures the French longitudinal churn rate the locus asked for — this remains the single biggest open gap.

## Open questions

- The exact French bureau-count-stability rate across 2022→2024 scrutins is unmeasured; the protocol above (compare bureau counts per commune across `id_election` values in the aggregated dataset) is cheap to run directly on already-published data and should be treated as a required first engineering step for this project, not a nice-to-have.
- Whether INSEE's REU refresh cycle (every 5 years, next likely ~2027) will produce an updated `table-bv-reu.csv` before the 2027 présidentielle, or whether the September 2022 snapshot will still be the only available REU-side crosswalk at that point, is not addressed anywhere in the corpus — this materially affects whether REU-based socio-demographic overlays are usable at all for 2027 without further staleness caveats.
- The `decouplage_bv` function in mapvotr's R source was not read directly (would show exactly how INSEE's own tooling detects/handles bureau splits) — a follow-up read of that function would sharpen the mechanism argument.
- No GitHub issue thread was found that discusses ID mismatches specifically in `datagouv/bureau-vote`'s issue tracker (it had only a Renovate-bot issue); the discussion evidence came entirely from the data.gouv.fr dataset discussions tab instead.
