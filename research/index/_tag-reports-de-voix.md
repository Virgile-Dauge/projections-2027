- [[peut-on-faire-confiance-aux-sondages-politiques-un-statisticien-nous-donne-les-c]] — Peut-on faire confiance aux sondages politiques ? Un statisticien nous donne les clés pour les décrypter — The Conversation (statistician op-ed, 2027 presidential context) on the general methodological reliability of French electoral survey estimates. Central concept: 'communauté de biais' (community of bias) — when multiple institutes use similar quota-based methods and similar redressment assumptions (notably vote-transfer-between-blocs adjustments), a shared systematic error can emerge across institutes; convergence/stability of published figures is 'not a guarantee of reliability, but the symptom of collective confidence in a collective error.' Cites the 2024 legislative polling failure (institutes projected RN+allies at 170-230 seats vs actual 143) as direct evidence. Notes France's quota-sampling method has no rigorous mathematical basis for 'margin of error' claims (per Pascal Ardilly/INSEE testimony to the Sénat, 2010 report no.54) — real total error (bias + variance) in the 2012 presidential election was empirically 4-6 points vs displayed theoretical margins of 1.8-2.7 points. Legally, the 'sondage' (voting-intention survey) is regulated by the Commission des sondages under loi n°2016-508, but the 'projection en sièges' (seat projection, dependent on transfer/report-de-voix assumptions across 577 local races) is explicitly NOT controlled or certified by that commission. Directly relevant: the piece frames vote-transfer-between-blocs assumptions as a specific, named source of correlated/systematic (not just random) polling error. `evergreen`
