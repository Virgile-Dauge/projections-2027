From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
**Uniform national swing** , or **UNS** is a system for translating [opinion polls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion_poll "Opinion poll"), which give overall vote proportions, to expected eventual parliamentary seats in a [constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituency "Constituency") based [first past the post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_past_the_post "First past the post") system, as in the [UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK "UK") general elections. Under the uniform national swing, changes in the vote proportions ([swing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swing_\(politics\) "Swing \(politics\)")) since the previous election are assumed to be constant across all constituencies. By applying this change to the previous per-constituency based vote proportions, a prediction for which party would have the most votes in each constituency may be made, which is then counted and totalled for a final seat count.
Alternative methods exist, such as the proportional national swing system. According to [Peter Kellner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Kellner "Peter Kellner"), UNS has a mixed record of success at predicting seat counts in the UK.
