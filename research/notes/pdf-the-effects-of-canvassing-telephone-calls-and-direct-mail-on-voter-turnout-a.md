## Abstract 
This research examines the impact of various methods of voter outreach—specifically canvassing, telephone calls, and direct mail—on voter turnout through a field experiment. The hypothesis posits that personal canvassing is more effective in mobilizing voters compared to other outreach methods. The findings reveal that personal contact significantly increases voter turnout, with statistical analysis indicating a measurable turnout differential when compared to other methods of contact.

## Key takeaways 
  1. Personal canvassing increases voter turnout by 9.8 percentage points, emphasizing its effectiveness over other methods.
  2. Telephone calls did not improve turnout and may have slightly decreased it compared to the control group.
  3. Direct mail raised turnout by 0.6 percentage points per mailing, suggesting limited effectiveness.
  4. The study involved 29,380 registered voters in New Haven, with 5,794 assigned to personal canvassing.
  5. Findings highlight a decline in face-to-face political activity correlating with lower voter turnout rates.
